The quiet details.

  • Pixel-matched to your notch — height, width, and corner curves measured from the system, exact on every notched Mac
  • No notch, no problem — on other Macs and external displays it hangs from the top edge as its own little island
  • Closed means closed — the island ignores your mouse entirely; clicks land on whatever is behind it
  • Never steals focus — the panel is non-activating; it only takes the keyboard while you type in it
  • Every morning, a clean slate — the day flips at 4 a.m., not midnight, so a 1 a.m. finish still counts
  • Reminders stay polite — they replace, never stack, and respect Focus modes
  • Launch at login — one toggle, no daemon
  • No accounts, no telemetry — your data never leaves the Mac

Common questions.

Is the download safe to open?

Yes — releases are signed with an Apple Developer ID certificate and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens Daily Goal without any warnings. Prefer to see for yourself? It’s open source — build it in about ten seconds.

Doesn’t it get in the way?

No — it lives in the notch, the one part of the screen nothing else uses, and while closed it ignores your mouse completely: clicks land on the menu bar and windows behind it. It only becomes real when you rest your pointer on the notch — and closes again the moment you leave. A cursor merely passing the top of the screen doesn’t open it.

My Mac has no notch. Does this still work?

Yes — on Macs without a notch (and on external displays) the island hangs from the top edge of the screen as its own little Dynamic Island, same behavior, same look. On notched MacBooks it measures the real housing from the system, so the fit is pixel-exact on every model.

How do the reminders behave?

On the schedule you pick (default: every hour), while today’s goal is still open. At the Mac, the notch opens and glows for a moment — no notification clutter. Away for a few minutes, and it posts one macOS notification that waits in Notification Center; a fresh reminder replaces the old one instead of stacking.

They honor Focus modes, go quiet the moment you finish, and Remind Me → Off in the menu bar turns them off entirely.

Where does my data live?

In UserDefaults on your Mac — the goal, the streak, and a 90-day history. Nothing to sign into, no telemetry. Reminders are local notifications, generated on your Mac. The app’s only network call asks GitHub for the latest version number — at most every 8 hours, it sends nothing about you, and you can switch it off in the menu bar. Updates are never installed behind your back: only when you click Install does the new version download from GitHub, and it must match the release’s published SHA-256 checksum and carry the developer’s Apple-verified signature before it replaces the app.

Why does the day end at 4 a.m.?

Because your day ends when you sleep, not when the calendar says so. Finish something at half past midnight and it still counts — the slate wipes while you’re in bed.

What does it cost?

Nothing, ever. It’s MIT-licensed open source — use it, fork it, ship your own.
